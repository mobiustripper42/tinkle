that has been latching since Sep 5, with Eric at the box and a multimeter.

**Next Steps:**

- **No urgent work.** Season is over; a latched fault costs close to nothing until spring.
- **Issue #110 (clean rebuild on a single protoboard) is the chosen fix.** Eric's call:
  replace the prototype jumper nest with a hardened build over the winter rather than
  chase the intermittent further.
- **Rebuild design input — add a freewheel diode across the pump.** Cathode to pump `+`,
  anode to pump `−`, sized for the pump's full running current with inrush headroom.
  There is none today: the BOM's TVS clamps are drain–source per FET for the valves plus
  one across the 24V rail (`docs/DRAFT-v1.4-BOM.md:45, 61`), and the relay modules'
  flyback is on the coil, protecting the module's own transistor, not the contacts
  (`docs/DRAFT-v1.4-BOM.md:46`). Not yet filed as an issue or a decision.
- **Next time it fails, grab one reading before wiggling anything:** contact drop from
  `COM` to `NO` on each relay board, during a run with the pump drawing current. Tens of
  millivolts = healthy; hundreds of millivolts or volts = that contact is the failure.
- Issues #185 (nightly reboot refuses to reboot while faulted) and #184 (nothing alerts
  on a fault) target next spring's season start, not now.

**Context:**

- **Measured, this session, at the box:**
  - PSU 24.12 V.
  - Run active, pump *disconnected*: 20 V at the pump connector.
  - Run active, pump *connected*: **0 V at the pump terminals.**
  - Safety relay `NO` read 0 V; a later probe of the pump relay read 24 V — contradictory
    under the documented wiring, and best explained by the fault flickering between the
    two readings.
  - Pump runs correctly on a 12 V bench supply — slow, but good pressure.
  - Wiring confirmed as-built matches `docs/tinkle_buildup.md:392`:
    `24V+` → safety relay `COM` → safety `NO` → pump relay `COM` → pump `NO` → pump `+`;
    pump `−` → GND.
- **The key deduction:** the 20 V open-circuit reading was phantom. The pump demonstrably
  runs at 12 V, so a real 20 V would have spun it. A DMM's ~10 MΩ input reads a healthy
  voltage through a connection that cannot pass amps. The 0 V under load confirmed it.
- **Relay LEDs and clicks report the coil, not the contacts** — the modules have their own
  coil driver and flyback (`docs/DRAFT-v1.4-BOM.md:46`). An eroded or carbonized contact
  clicks and lights exactly like a healthy one. This is the blind spot issue #131
  (armed-rail witness) exists to close.
- **It started working again after everything was stuffed back in the box.** Handling
  changed something, which implicates crimps and the jumper nest as much as the relay
  contacts. Both are covered by the #110 rebuild.
- **The flow sensor is probably fine.** Sep 18's zero pulses were zero pulses because zero
  water moved. The earlier sensor theory was built on the nightly-reboot timestamp being a
  coincidence next to contacts that were failing anyway.
- **Three theories died this investigation** before the meter settled it: safety-relay
  re-arm between chained runs (killed by Sep 18 being a 1st run), catchment tank drawdown
  (killed by rainfall data — Sep 16 was the month's wettest day and it faulted the next
  morning), and the flow sensor (killed by 0 V at the pump).
